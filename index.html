From a pavement stall at the crossroads

In 1968, Santosh Kumar Jain came to Lucknow from Tikait Nagar, a small town in Barabanki district, and set up a chaat stall on the pavement near the Lalbagh crossroads. There was no shop and no signboard: just a stall, a tawa, and a spot between Novelty Cinema and the GPO where the whole city seemed to pass by every evening.

The stall earned its place the slow way. Regulars came back for the tikki, brought their children, and eventually their children's children. In time the pavement stall became a proper unit, a small standing-room counter that still works the way the stall did: you order, you watch your plate being made, and you eat it right there or carry it home.

Today the shop is run by Santosh Kumar Jain's sons, Sanjeev Kumar Jain and Raju. Sanjeev still prepares the house seasoning himself. The sweet curd, the tamarind chutney and the dry spice mix that finish every plate are made to the same hand, every day, before the counter opens.

No onion. No garlic.

As a Jain-run kitchen, everything here is made without two things most people assume chaat can't do without. It never held the flavour back. The seasoning does the work instead, and after five decades the queue outside settles the argument.

What people praise

The same three things, across platforms and years: the flavour and freshness of the plates, the balance of the house seasoning, and the quick, friendly service at the counter. Many reviewers call it the best chaat shop in Lucknow outright.

What to know before you come

The honest note in the reviews: there's no seating, and the counter gets crowded at peak evening hours. It's a standing-room shop and always has been. Come as you are, eat where you stand, or take it home.
